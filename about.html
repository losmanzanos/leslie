About Leslie

Forty years of helping adults find their way home to themselves.

For over four decades, I’ve had the privilege of helping people heal from childhood trauma and rediscover the authentic self that often had to be hidden in order to survive, to be loved, or to belong.

I’m continually awed and inspired by my clients’ courage, resilience, and determination to grow beyond the pain of the past.

Training & Background

A lifelong study of how people heal.

My journey as a therapist began after earning my MSW at Columbia University, followed by extensive post-graduate work at New York’s analytic institutes, including the Psychoanalytic Institute for Clinical Social Work. Education has been a life-long passion and continues to this day — with advanced training in trauma-informed treatment, self-psychology, CBT, and psychodynamic approaches that together help uncover both the practical tools and the deeper roots of emotional pain and lasting change.

Early in my career I developed the first women’s therapy program at the Brooklyn Veterans Administration, consulted for at-risk youth programs in New York City, and provided individual therapy in private practice.

After moving to Florida in 1991, I served as Clinical Supervisor for the Jewish Family Services of Greater Miami and continued my specialty in private practice — helping adults from diverse cultures and backgrounds heal from childhood trauma and create lives that feel authentic and whole.

My Approach

Therapy is a deeply personal journey.

At the heart of my approach is empathy. Healing happens in a space where you feel safe, understood, and truly accepted — that kind of connection opens the way to explore and process the wounds of childhood trauma, and makes room for the real, true you to emerge.

My approach is grounded in the belief that no matter what someone has been through, every person has a true self — a core essence: the part that feels alive, vital, emotionally aware, and able to connect. It’s the part that knows, “this is me — real, grounded, and okay as I am.” It’s our natural clarity, our real needs and feelings, values, and inner steadiness.

It’s also the part that often had to be hidden in order to survive, to be loved, or to belong.

In therapy we create a relationship where all parts are welcome. As trust builds, old patterns begin to loosen and the real true you can gently emerge at your own pace — you start to heal and reconnect with yourself, and that begins to transform your life.
